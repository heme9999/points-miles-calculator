# What is Cents Per Point (CPP) and How to Calculate It?

If you are diving into the world of points and miles, the term **CPP** is everywhere. But what exactly does it mean?

## CPP Definition

CPP stands for **Cents Per Point**. It is a metric used to determine how much cash value you are getting out of every single point or mile you redeem. Since different points currencies have different values, CPP helps standardize comparisons.

## Cents vs Dollars Math

Since cash prices are usually displayed in dollars and points are in the thousands, calculating CPP requires converting dollars to cents. 
$1 = 100 cents.

## The Basic Formula

The simplest formula for calculating CPP is:
**CPP = (Cash Price in Cents) / (Points Required)**

For example, a $200 hotel room that costs 10,000 points:
CPP = 20,000 cents / 10,000 points = 2.0 CPP.

## The Tax-Inclusive Formula

When booking award flights, you often still have to pay taxes and fees. To get an accurate CPP, you must subtract these out-of-pocket costs from the cash price.

**CPP = ((Cash Price - Taxes & Fees) * 100) / Points Required**

## Airline vs Hotel Differences

* **Hotels:** When you book a hotel with points, standard taxes and fees are almost always waived. Sometimes resort fees are waived too (like with Hilton and Hyatt).
* **Airlines:** You always have to pay some amount of cash for taxes and fees on award flights. This can range from $5.60 on a US domestic flight to over $800 in fuel surcharges on a British Airways business class ticket.

## Avoid Using Inflated Cash Prices

A common pitfall is using inflated or unrealistic cash list prices to calculate CPP. If a First Class flight lists for $15,000 in cash, but you would normally not purchase that ticket with cash, using that full price distorts the practical value saved. Calculate CPP based on the cash price you would have reasonably paid for the journey.
