# How Much Are Airline Miles Worth?

Trying to figure out the exact value of an airline mile is like trying to hit a moving target. Unlike cash, miles are a proprietary currency controlled by the airlines, and their value changes constantly.

## Miles Have No Fixed Cash Value

Unless you are cashing out points directly through a travel portal at a fixed rate (like Chase Sapphire Reserve at 1.5 cents each), airline miles have no fixed cash value. Their value is determined entirely by how you redeem them. 

## Editorial vs. Actual Value

Many travel blogs publish "mile valuations," stating that a Delta SkyMile is worth 1.2 cents, or an Air France Flying Blue mile is worth 1.5 cents. These are **editorial valuations**—averages based on typical use cases. 

Your **actual value** depends solely on the specific flight you book. You could easily get 0.9 cents of value from a mile, or 5.0 cents, on the exact same airline.

## Economy vs. Business vs. Short-Haul

The value you get out of your miles depends heavily on what you book:
* **Economy Class:** Usually yields a lower CPP (around 1.0 to 1.5 cents). Cash prices are generally low, so the points don't save you as much.
* **Business/First Class:** Usually yields a high CPP (often 3.0 to 8.0+ cents). Because cash prices for premium cabins are astronomically high, using points provides immense outsized value.
* **Short-Haul Flights:** Booking last-minute, expensive short-haul flights using distance-based award charts (like British Airways Avios) is a classic way to extract huge value for economy tickets.

## Avoid Universal Fixed Valuations

Don't let universal valuations dictate your bookings. If a blog says a mile is worth 2.0 cents, and you want to book a flight that only yields 1.4 cents per point, it might still be a great deal for *you* if it saves you $500 out of pocket.
