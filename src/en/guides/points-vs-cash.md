# Points vs Cash: Should I Use Points or Pay Cash?

When planning a trip, one of the most common questions is: **Should I use points or pay cash?** There is no one-size-fits-all answer, but you can make an informed decision by following a simple framework.

## The Decision Framework

Choosing between points and cash comes down to getting the most value out of your hard-earned rewards while protecting your wallet. Ask yourself these questions:
1. Do I have enough cash on hand to comfortably pay for this?
2. Does the points redemption offer good value compared to my baseline valuation?
3. What are the cancellation policies for cash vs. points?

## The CPP Method (Cents Per Point)

The easiest way to compare points and cash is by calculating the **Cents Per Point (CPP)**. 
Divide the cash price of the flight or hotel (minus any taxes and fees you still have to pay on an award ticket) by the number of points required.

If the calculated CPP is higher than your personal valuation of those points, book with points. If it's lower, pay cash.

## Don't Forget Taxes and Fees

Award flights aren't entirely free. You will still have to pay government taxes and, depending on the airline, carrier-imposed surcharges. Sometimes these surcharges can be hundreds of dollars, significantly reducing the value of your points. Always subtract these taxes from the cash price when calculating CPP.

## Forgone Value (Opportunity Cost)

When you pay with points, you aren't earning points on that flight or hotel stay. Paying cash usually earns you miles and elite qualifying metrics. You should factor in this "forgone value" when comparing the two options. 

## Flexibility and Cancellation

Points bookings are often highly flexible. Many airline and hotel loyalty programs allow you to cancel award bookings and redeposit your points for free or for a very low fee. In contrast, the cheapest cash fares (like Basic Economy) are typically non-refundable.

## 3 Examples

### Example 1: High Value Redemption
* Cash Price: $1,200
* Award Cost: 30,000 miles + $50 taxes
* CPP: ($1,200 - $50) / 30,000 = 3.83 cpp
* **Verdict:** Use points!

### Example 2: Low Value Redemption
* Cash Price: $150
* Award Cost: 15,000 miles + $10 taxes
* CPP: ($150 - $10) / 15,000 = 0.93 cpp
* **Verdict:** Pay cash and save your points.

### Example 3: The Surcharge Trap
* Cash Price: $800
* Award Cost: 40,000 miles + $500 taxes
* CPP: ($800 - $500) / 40,000 = 0.75 cpp
* **Verdict:** Pay cash. The high surcharges ruin the value.
