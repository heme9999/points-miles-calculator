# Transfer Bonus Break-Even Calculation Example

Credit card ecosystems (like Chase Ultimate Rewards or Amex Membership Rewards) periodically offer transfer bonuses to specific airline or hotel partners. For example, instead of a standard 1:1 transfer, you might get a 20% or 30% bonus. 

This example breaks down how these bonuses affect your point balance and how to determine if the transfer makes sense for a specific redemption.

## The Flight Scenario

Imagine you want to book a one-way business class flight to Europe.

*   **Cash Price of the Ticket:** $2,500
*   **Airline Miles Required:** 60,000 miles
*   **Taxes & Fees on Award Ticket:** $150
*   **Base Transfer Ratio:** 1:1

## Standard Transfer (No Bonus)

Without a bonus, you need to transfer the exact amount of miles required:
*   **Credit Card Points Required:** 60,000 points
*   **Redemption Value (CPP):** ($2,500 - $150) / 60,000 × 100 = **3.91 CPP**

*Your credit card points are worth 3.91 cents each for this specific redemption.*

## With a 20% Transfer Bonus

If your credit card is offering a 20% transfer bonus to this specific airline:
*   **Calculation:** 60,000 miles / 1.20 = 50,000 points
*   **Credit Card Points Required:** 50,000 points
*   **Redemption Value (CPP):** ($2,500 - $150) / 50,000 × 100 = **4.70 CPP**

*By utilizing the 20% bonus, you save 10,000 credit card points, boosting your redemption value from 3.91 CPP to 4.70 CPP.*

## With a 30% Transfer Bonus

If your credit card is offering a 30% transfer bonus to this specific airline:
*   **Calculation:** 60,000 miles / 1.30 = 46,153.8 points
*   **Credit Card Points Required:** 47,000 points (since most programs require transfers in increments of 1,000)
*   **Redemption Value (CPP):** ($2,500 - $150) / 47,000 × 100 = **5.00 CPP**

## Break-Even Analysis & Caveats

Just because there is a transfer bonus does not mean the redemption is automatically a good deal. 

If the same flight only cost $300 in cash, your redemption value (even with a 30% bonus) would be a dismal **0.31 CPP** (($300 - $150) / 47,000). You are much better off paying cash and saving your flexible credit card points.

**Crucial Warning:**
*   **Transfers are irreversible:** Once you transfer points from a flexible credit card program to an airline or hotel, you cannot transfer them back.
*   **Do not transfer speculatively:** Only transfer points when you have verified that award space exists for your specific dates.
