# Buying Points With a 100% Bonus Example

Loyalty programs frequently run promotions offering a "100% Bonus" when you purchase points or miles. While doubling your points sounds like an incredible deal, the true test is whether the cost per point is lower than the value you can extract from them.

## The Promotional Offer

Suppose an airline is selling miles with a 100% bonus promotion.

*   **Base Points to Purchase:** 50,000 miles
*   **Bonus Points (100%):** 50,000 miles
*   **Total Points Received:** 100,000 miles
*   **Purchase Price:** $1,500
*   **Taxes & Processing Fees:** $0

## Calculating Your Cost Per Point

To find out if this is a good deal, we must calculate the exact cost per point you are paying:

*   **Cost Per Point:** ($1,500 / 100,000 miles) × 100 = **1.50 Cents Per Point (CPP)**

Your **break-even point** is 1.50 CPP. This means you must redeem these miles for *at least* 1.50 CPP to avoid losing money compared to just buying a cash ticket. 

## When Is It Worth It?

If you are planning to book an international business class flight that costs $4,000 in cash, but only requires 100,000 miles (plus $200 in taxes), your redemption value would be:
*   **Redemption Value:** ($4,000 - $200) / 100,000 × 100 = **3.80 CPP**

Since your redemption value (3.80 CPP) is much higher than your purchase cost (1.50 CPP), buying the miles for $1,500 saves you $2,300. In this specific scenario, buying points is highly advantageous.

## Key Risks to Consider
 
*   **Award Availability:** Avoid buying points speculatively. Ensure the exact flight or hotel room you want is available for award booking *before* you make the purchase.
*   **Posting Times:** Some purchased points post quickly, while others can take up to 72 hours. During this delay, award space might change.
*   **Non-Refundable:** Point purchases are almost always processed by third parties (like Points.com) and are generally non-refundable.
*   **Devaluation Risk:** If you buy points without an immediate redemption plan, loyalty programs may adjust award pricing in the future.
